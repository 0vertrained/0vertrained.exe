# Race the Climb

**Product and implementation plan for 0vertrained.** Design baseline `cc5dfce`.

## Using the first release

Open **Train → Race the Climb** and select the bundled climb for a first session. To use an outdoor course, import its GPX or select a permitted timestamped Apple Health route on the rebuilt iPhone app. Choose the climb, a pacer or eligible recorded effort, then start recording or schedule the run in Today. Previewing the course keeps the training log untouched. Saved course packs and measured attempt traces stay on the device. The account log carries the compact workout result.

The Santa Fe Striders publish a GPX with two tracks for the Big Tesuque Trail Run. Save it to Files, choose the first segment for the climb from Aspen Vista, and pick a target pacer. The organizer’s file contains elevations but no timestamps. The Philmont organizer also links a Gaia map with GPX export for its Tooth Ridge Sprint near Cimarron. [Big Tesuque course and download](https://runsignup.com/Race/NM/SantaFe/TheBigT) · [Philmont course guide](https://runsignup.com/Race/PhilmontTrailRace/Page/ToothRidgeSprint)

The iPhone native bridge reads supported treadmill telemetry. The versioned web runner uses fresh samples, journals progress locally and detects uncertain crossings after gaps. Personal course terrain downloads use an explicit location-area request to USGS. The bundled sample and profile view work without terrain service access. Model-specific automatic incline remains gated on physical qualification. Use manual incline cues on current production hardware. Install the updated iPhone and Watch apps through Xcode for Bluetooth, Health imports and Watch recording.

### Check a gym treadmill

In the rebuilt iPhone app, open **Train → Race the Climb → Treadmill connection**. Wake the console and tap **Find treadmills** to find machines that offer the Bluetooth Fitness Machine Service (FTMS). For a Peloton, **Look for Peloton** also finds nearby broadcasts with Peloton, Tread, or FitSwitch in the name. Tap your machine to check its services. A name match identifies a candidate, and an empty scan means the phone saw no matching broadcast during that search.

You can run this check before starting a workout. As you use the treadmill, compare the speed, distance and grade on the console with the last values and their ages in the report. The incline verdict uses the machine’s target-incline flag and its Bluetooth control point. Moving the deck with its knob can produce a grade reading even on a machine that accepts no incline commands from apps. The report sends no movement commands. After disconnecting, use **Copy compatibility report** to keep the result before you scan again or leave Race the Climb.

The sections below describe the full product design and physical release criteria.

## The workout worth building

An athlete opens last year's trail race, selects the climb from the creek crossing to the ridge, and taps **Rehearse indoors**. At the gym, their belt distance moves them through that climb. A ghost moves through the same switchbacks according to the original race recording. On a compatible treadmill the deck follows the terrain. On another machine the athlete can follow incline cues.

At the crest they see **“18 seconds ahead of your outdoor run. You lost 6 seconds before the hairpin and gained 24 after it.”** They can replay that comparison or schedule the hairpin-to-crest section for next week. The experience succeeds if they can choose a meaningful climb, run it without handling a phone, and identify one section they want to practice again.

## The athlete journey

| Moment | Experience | What the athlete controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | **Train → Race rehearsal → Race the Climb**. An eligible outdoor activity in History also offers **Race this climb**. A bundled sample with a named pacer works on first launch. | Try the sample, select a saved course, open a past run, or import a GPX. |
| Select the climb | A linked map and elevation profile have start and crest handles. Moving either highlights the same stretch on both views. Show distance, ascent, steepest sustained grade and landmark splits. Offer candidate climbs when elevation quality permits. | Trim the segment, choose direction, name landmarks, or use the whole route. Reversing direction requires a matching reference or a pacer. |
| Pick a reference | Offer the original outdoor effort, a previous comparable indoor attempt, a target pacer, or a solo run. Show the date, how the reference was measured, and whether timing covers the entire climb. | Choose a ghost and preview its splits. An old checkpoint-only result can supply split targets. |
| Schedule | Save the climb to the athlete's library. **Run now**, **schedule as my run**, and **keep for later** are separate actions. Today opens a scheduled climb directly. | On a two-session day, attach it explicitly to the PM run. A logged lift stays on its original session. |
| Set up | Find the selected treadmill or choose manual cues. Show the source of distance, available incline range, and what fraction of the course that machine can reproduce. Offer a short course download before entering a gym with poor reception. | Select the specific machine, set a personal grade cap, select units and cues, and separately enable automatic incline for this session. |
| Warm up and start | The warm-up can count toward the indoor workout. The climb and its ghost start together when the athlete confirms the start line. A stationary belt holds progress. | Start and adjust belt speed at the console. Enter the climb from the phone or paired Watch. |
| Run | The phone shows the ridge, runner, ghost and next landmark. The Watch gives a short cue before a material grade change or split. Screen-reader and reduced-motion modes offer the same workout with a fixed profile. | See the time gap, take a station stop, turn off auto incline immediately, mute secondary cues, or finish early. |
| Finish and repeat | Show the crest result, interval-by-interval gains and losses, how much of the terrain the deck reproduced, and any gaps in measurement. A cooldown can continue in the same indoor workout. | Save, repeat a selected section, or attach a repeat to a future run. A partial attempt can be saved to the last verified position. |

## What works on each setup

| Setup | Course progress | Incline and recording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browser or phone preview | Athlete scrubs the course or uses a simulated pace. | Shows terrain and reference playback. Preview activity stays outside the training log. |

Alternatively, the athlete enters console readings at splits and can use a manually entered pace estimate between them. | Phone or Watch gives manual grade cues. Until the next reading, estimated progress and gap are visibly provisional. With split readings alone, show a split clock. |
| Treadmill with readable telemetry | Prefer a verified belt-distance counter. Where available, integrate fresh measured belt speed with a monotonic clock as a labeled estimate. | Enables live terrain and ghost progress. Show measured incline only when the machine actually reports it. |

With the iPhone alone, a real session can still be saved to the app log. Downloaded courses, ghost playback, live local telemetry and pending results should work in airplane mode with Bluetooth enabled. Online lookup, new maps and account sync can resume later.

## Course and ghost engineering

**Build a durable course library alongside the existing rehearsal editor.** Today, `src/rehearsal/course.js` produces a schedule-friendly line of up to 450 points and 40 checkpoints. It keeps latitude and longitude. Existing Watch attempts retain checkpoint times and completion distance. Keep those formats readable. Add a higher-resolution, immutable course revision for the simulator and send a compact cue list to the Watch. Replacing next Friday's rehearsal must leave last month's attempts replayable.

1. **Ingest the best available source.** Retain available coordinates, elevation, timestamps, segment boundaries and waypoint names before simplifying a GPX. Compute course distance from the coordinates. Let the athlete select a continuous segment from a multi-segment file. A hand-drawn course supplies geometry. A GPX track with reliable timestamps can also supply a personal ghost. In a later increment, the native run importer should request an associated HealthKit route on demand, accumulate its location batches, and finish the import only when the query completes. Handle a route arriving after its workout summary. [GPX 1.1 schema](https://www.topografix.com/gpx/1/1/) · [Apple workout route queries](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/reading-route-data)
2. **Establish one distance axis.** Sum geodesic horizontal lengths along the ordered route in metres. Store the selected start and crest as distances along that revision. Rebase both athletes’ distance and time to zero at the chosen start line, including when it lies midway through the original race. Match an outdoor trace by location, direction, continuity and plausible forward progress. Crossing the outbound trail on the return leg must never jump the athlete to the crest. Preserve gaps and uncertain matches. Allow an athlete to correct a start or finish alignment before saving the reference.
3. **Build the grade profile.** Prefer a sound continuous elevation source for the segment. Where elevation is missing, sample a documented digital elevation model with known coverage and resolution. Track provenance, vertical datum and uncertainty. Smooth elevation over horizontal distance, then calculate percent grade as 100 × rise / horizontal run. A provisional 20–40 m smoothing window is a field-test parameter. Flag spikes, gaps, bridges and tunnels. Show uncertain terrain in the preview and suspend actuator guidance across those stretches. A flat line inferred from missing data would misrepresent the course. [USGS elevation resolution and datums](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-projection-horizontal-datum-vertical-datum-and-resolution-a-usgs-digital-elevation-model)
4. **Build the reference clock.** A personal ghost needs a timestamped progress trace or a recorded indoor attempt on the same revision. Match an outside recording once and store a time-to-distance curve with quality flags. Preserve true aid-station dwell and distinguish it from missing GPS or a paused recorder. Label large gaps and limit comparison to supported splits when confidence drops. If the source contains only moving time or has unknown pause semantics, offer moving-time practice and label it accordingly. Elapsed race comparisons require a compatible reference clock. A synthetic pacer is clearly identified wherever its result appears.
5. **Package terrain for the gym.** Store the elevation profile, course line, reference curve, landmarks and a lightweight terrain mesh locally. To model the surrounding ridge, sample a corridor of elevation data on both sides of the trail. If only the route’s centerline elevations are available, offer the real profile and a course ribbon until terrain coverage is available. Use a 3D terrain renderer on capable phones and a fixed 2D profile on older devices or under thermal pressure. Keep an attribution manifest and a size estimate per download. Use app-generated terrain or a source licensed for offline distribution. The current editor's public OpenStreetMap raster service explicitly prohibits bulk offline downloads. [MapLibre 3D terrain example](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/3d-terrain/) · [OpenStreetMap tile policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/)

### A fair race clock

One accepted belt metre advances one virtual course metre. Store that convention with the attempt. Keep virtual course ascent, measured deck incline and estimated physical climbing as separate quantities. Unsupported declines, an athlete's incline cap, and differences between an indoor and an outdoor surface belong in the result.

Start with **elapsed-time comparison**. Both clocks start at the chosen start line. The reference reproduces its recorded stops. If the athlete rests, their elapsed comparison clock continues and course progress holds. Track moving time separately. An interruption with unknown distance produces an explicit gap. A later confirmed counter reading can resolve distance, but intermediate switchback times remain unknown. At a station, keep first arrival and departure separately so dwelling never changes the recorded arrival split.

At a shared landmark, **cumulative lead = reference elapsed − athlete elapsed**. A positive result means the athlete arrived ahead. The change in lead between two landmarks is the gain or loss on that section. Interpolate crossings only between trustworthy samples.

| Landmark | Outdoor reference | Indoor attempt | Lead at landmark | Gain on this section |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Start | 0:00 | 0:00 | Even | — |
| Hairpin | 3:00 | 3:06 | 6 s behind | Lost 6 s |
| Crest | 7:00 | 6:42 | 18 s ahead | Gained 24 s |

On the live screen, place the ghost at the distance it reached after the athlete's elapsed time. Display the time gap at the athlete's current course position. Handle a faster ghost reaching the crest first by leaving it at the finish and keeping the athlete's clock running. Keep indoor bests on matching course revisions and comparable assistance settings. Outdoor race records retain their original activity classification.

## Treadmill integration and failure behavior

Build a small Swift Core Bluetooth controller in the iPhone app, independent of the WHOOP connection. The iPhone owns the control session. The Watch and Capacitor UI consume snapshots and send athlete intents to the controller. Keep belt start, stop and speed with the physical console. A machine advertising Bluetooth or FTMS earns discovery, then its reported features and tested behavior determine the modes to offer. The Bluetooth Fitness Machine Service defines separate capabilities for speed and inclination targets. [Bluetooth Fitness Machine Service](https://www.bluetooth.com/specifications/specs/fitness-machine-service-1-0-1/) · [Bluetooth SIG conformance tests](https://files.bluetooth.com/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2024/10/FTMS.TS_.p6.pdf)

| State or event | Controller behavior | Athlete sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery and pairing | Show nearby candidates and bind only the machine the athlete selects. Read features, incline range and step. Subscribe to treadmill telemetry, machine status and control-point indications. Remember a friendly device name. | Machine identity, available readings and **Manual cues** or **Auto incline available**. |
| Arming | Require a qualified model and firmware, fresh distance and inclination telemetry, a running belt, user opt-in and successful control acquisition. Keep a single command in flight. | Current deck grade, cap and a persistent **Use manual incline** action. |
| Following terrain | Limit target to the machine and user range, round to a supported increment and rate-limit the change. Use only measured actuator lag for bounded lookahead. Distinguish a successful Bluetooth write from machine acceptance and physical response. | Upcoming course grade and actual deck grade. A capped or flattened section is labeled. |
| Console override, unexpected response, stale readings, safety-key event or lost control | Immediately disarm, invalidate the command generation and discard queued requests. An already transmitted command can still complete. Give console changes priority. | A cue to check the deck and use the console. The app can keep recording whatever remains measurable. |
| Phone locks, backgrounds, dies, or loses Bluetooth | Suspend automatic writes. Resume discovery or read-only telemetry where the OS permits. Rebase counters after reconnect. Old commands expire across process restarts. | The last measured incline with its timestamp. Automatic incline requires an explicit re-arm. The deck may still be at its previous incline. |
| Finish or change athlete | Disarm before closing the session. Close the appropriate writer, persist its receipt and release the connection. | **Climb finished** or **Workout saved**, as appropriate. Ending the climb in the app leaves belt operation with the console. |

Decode flags and units explicitly, especially percent incline versus ramp angle and cumulative distance versus instantaneous speed. Use a monotonic clock for sensor integration. A reset or wrapped counter, a stale packet, a zero-speed stop, and a new connection are distinct events. When handing off from belt to Watch distance, anchor the new source at the last verified position and start a new quality interval. A source change cannot credit the same metres twice. Opening the web app, reconnecting WHOOP, or reopening a saved session must never arm a treadmill. [Apple Core Bluetooth background behavior](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/NetworkingInternetWeb/Conceptual/CoreBluetooth_concepts/CoreBluetoothBackgroundProcessingForIOSApps/PerformingTasksWhileYourAppIsInTheBackground.html) · [Apple state restoration and relaunch rules](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3115-bluetooth-state-restoration-app-relaunch-rules)

## Screens and feedback in motion

- **Course preview.** A restrained terrain view and elevation profile share one selection. Show the first grade change, the highest sustained grade and any mismatch with the paired deck. Use metric or imperial distance according to athlete preferences and percent for grade. A clearly labeled preview can be scrubbed without touching hardware.
- **Phone during the climb.** Large distance-to-crest and signed gap, next landmark, current deck grade, and one upcoming grade cue. Choose a steady follow camera or fixed overview. The runner follows distance samples. Camera animation can interpolate those samples for smoothness but can never change a timing result or send a machine command.
- **Watch during the climb.** One glance gives the gap and next actionable cue, such as “Hairpin in 80 m” or “Set incline to 5%.” Give manual adjustment cues once with useful lead time. During automatic control, suppress redundant wrist prompts. A loss-of-control cue takes priority over a split celebration. Support VoiceOver, Dynamic Type, large touch targets, haptic-only and audio-muted use. Pair every meaningful color with words or a symbol.
- **Debrief.** Lead with the crest result and the section with the largest verified change. Tap a switchback to replay both arrivals on the terrain. Show verified-distance coverage, reference type, stops, estimated stretches and the fraction of eligible distance run within the qualified incline tolerance. Describe unmeasured grades as unknown. **Repeat this section** creates a fresh attempt against a pinned reference.

## Architecture, ownership and privacy

| Component | Reuse | Proposed addition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Course library and Train screens | `src/rehearsal/`, the existing course editor and the Train entry point | Versioned imports, a segment picker, per-athlete downloads, a ghost picker and indoor results. Keep only a dated reference in the training plan. |
| Session engine | Current rehearsal checkpoint and split semantics | A deterministic reducer for belt or Watch samples, elapsed time, quality, station events and finish. Native Swift owns live indoor progress and journals measurements while a recording is active. The web preview uses the same event and clock rules with simulated inputs. Shared replay fixtures establish JS/Swift agreement. Both renderers read snapshots. |
| Native treadmill bridge | Existing Capacitor plugin registration patterns and independent wearable connections | Swift capability detection, telemetry decoding, explicit control state and a local recovery journal. Publish low-rate snapshots to the UI. Require every control intent to match the active owner, recording ID and current arm generation. Enforce capability and freshness checks in native code so a delayed view event or incompatible Netlify bundle cannot move the deck. Update the Bluetooth permission description, which currently names WHOOP. |
| Watch and HealthKit | `CardioWorkoutView.swift`, the workout mirror and athlete-scoped delivery receipts | An indoor-running mode with one HealthKit writer when the Watch records. Use the same recording ID on phone and Watch. The Watch already writes `OTCardioID`. Extend `RunsPlugin.swift` to return it and `src/watch/healthRuns.js` to reconcile it with the HealthKit UUID before generic matching. |
| Log and sync | Existing owner-scoped queues, daily entries and program-session IDs | Store one canonical workout and attach the climb analysis. A PM slot gets credit once. Account for late Health imports and phone/Watch finish events arriving in either order. Store the virtual route as course content. HealthKit records an indoor workout and its measured activity. |

**Minimum durable records**

- **Course revision** holds owner, course ID, revision/hash, geometry, distance, elevation source and quality, checkpoints, selected section bounds, distance convention, processing version and asset manifest.
- **Ghost revision** holds owner, course revision and direction, reference type and origin, time/distance samples or split anchors, stop intervals, coverage and timing policy. Imported route edits create a new revision for future sessions.
- **Recording and attempt** use separate IDs. The recording identifies the physical workout. Each timed climb gets a fresh attempt UUID linked to it, with owner, course and ghost revisions, start-day and optional training-slot ID, equipment mode, source changes, timestamps, checkpoints, measured and estimated stretches, applied limits, summary, HealthKit linkage and finalization state. Warm-up, timed climb and cooldown share the parent workout ID.
- **Control diagnostics** retain a short, local, bounded trace of requested grade, accepted target, measured grade, capability decisions and disarm reason. Make an explicit diagnostic export available to a tester. Keep precise location, raw Health traces and machine identifiers out of general analytics.

Persist active progress periodically and at every split or mode change. On relaunch, show **Resume recording** or **Finish partial**, with any missing interval identified. Preserve the stored owner and original start date across midnight. Stage the finished attempt locally before acknowledging delivery. Use account-scoped idempotent writes keyed by recording ID for the workout and attempt ID for its analysis. Duplicate finish messages, a reconnect on another day and a delayed Health import resolve to the same recording. Repeating the hairpin section during cooldown creates another timed attempt inside that workout. Repeating it tomorrow creates a new recording and attempt. Assign plan credit once per scheduled session.

Keep imported personal GPS and Health data local by default. Ship the first complete experience with local course packs and per-account result sync through the existing app path. Make any later cross-device storage of detailed personal packs a separate opt-in with access control, deletion and retention behavior specified before release. Removing a download frees local assets. Deleting a personal source offers removal of its trace and derived ghost, while the athlete can retain a past workout's splits and summary. Keep derived meshes and attribution together. Establish provider rights before enabling a new terrain region.

## Delivery sequence

Each milestone should yield something an athlete or hardware tester can use. The first four can run on an ordinary gym treadmill. Qualifying a particular model gates only its automatic mode.

| Milestone | Deliverable | Exit test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Prove one real climb | A race GPX supplied with permission, a trimmed climb, a timed outdoor reference, landmark markers and an offline phone replay. Open a separate hardware investigation with a candidate treadmill. | Hand-check the start, switchback sequence, crest, elapsed times and elevation profile against the source recording. Missing timestamps correctly offer a pacer or split mode. |
| 2. Make it a real indoor session | Read-only belt integration on one machine, Watch/manual fallback, one indoor recording, manual grade cues, pause, early finish, offline resume and idempotent save. | Run the climb at three belt speeds. The hill and landmarks arrive at the same route distances. Finish offline, reconnect twice, and verify one workout alongside the athlete's existing lift. |
| 3. Ship the visual and analytical payoff | 3D terrain, ghost playback, fixed-profile fallback, landmark deltas, quality labels and **Repeat this section**. | A tester can point to where they gained time, can explain a capped descent, and can start the chosen section again without editing the course. |
| 4. Make it a repeatable product | Library, past-run route import, Today/PM scheduling, saved setups, sample course, offline packs, Watch cues, accessibility, owner isolation and deletion. | A new athlete with no imported history can try the sample. A returning athlete can resume offline, use a different treadmill, and find an earlier result after editing the course. |
| 5. Qualify automatic incline | FTMS control, physical deck validation, explicit opt-in and a model/firmware compatibility list. | First test an unloaded deck against independent incline measurements, then supervised walking and varied running speeds. Pass every interruption case and document timing/error bounds for that model. |
| 6. Release in stages | Internal field build, opt-in gym beta, general availability for manual/read-only modes, and automatic incline enabled only for qualified combinations. | Review beta evidence against the gates below. Provide a way to disable automatic mode by model/firmware while preserving completed workouts and manual cues. |

**Planning assumption.** The first hardware decision is access to a specific treadmill and firmware for repeated tests. Start with one athlete-owned course, one physical iPhone/Watch pair and one treadmill that advertises incline control. Add a read-only gym machine and a plain treadmill before beta. An estimate for broad hardware coverage should follow observed results on those machines.

## Verification and release criteria

The numeric targets below are proposed engineering acceptance targets. Set hardware-specific incline tolerances from the measured deck response and the manufacturer's documented operating limits before runner testing.

| Test area | Proposed evidence and pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Math and replay | Golden fixtures for a sustained climb, switchbacks, overlapping outbound/return paths, gaps, pauses, aid-station dwell, repeated passes, and a partial finish. With clean 1 Hz input, split crossings must agree with hand-calculated truth within 1 second. Replaying identical input must produce identical final splits and quality labels. |
| Treadmill response | Test at three speeds. For a qualified model, document actual grade accuracy, response delay, supported increments and distance error at grade transitions. Test negative, capped and unsupported grades. Run with a separate inclinometer or comparable independent reference before trusting reported grade. Acknowledgment alone cannot pass this test. |
| Fault injection | Replay truncated packets, optional fields, counter resets, disconnects, delayed indications, safety-key removal, console changes, phone lock/kill, nearby duplicate device names and simultaneous WHOOP use. Require zero new incline requests after disarm and zero stale requests after reconnect. Verify console operation after each case. |
| Persistence and logging | Test Watch-first and phone-first delivery, HealthKit arriving later, offline finish, account change, partial restart, and a second real attempt on the same day. A continuous recording yields one workout and one opportunity for plan credit. Two timed repeats inside that recording yield two analyses. Two independently started workouts on the same course yield two workouts. |
| Usability and accessibility | Have beta athletes complete setup, identify the next cue, take manual control, finish and find their decisive split without coaching. Test small iPhone and Watch screens, large text, VoiceOver, reduced motion, noisy gyms and a mounted phone viewed at running distance. |
| Performance and privacy | Target smooth 30 fps terrain on the oldest supported test iPhone during a 60-minute session, with thermal fallback to the profile while timing stays correct. Verify downloaded-course playback with no network, lossless finalization under low storage or a clear retry state, and removal of local packs across sign-out/deletion. |

**First implementation slice.** Use one consented timestamped GPX and manually mark three landmarks. Build an importer that preserves the original samples, produce an immutable course/reference pair, and replay three synthetic belt-speed traces through the same progress engine. Verify the three splits by hand. In parallel, capture a read-only trace on the candidate treadmill and inventory its actual distance, incline, firmware and control capabilities. Those two deliverables tell the team whether the ghost is accurate enough and which hardware mode to build first.

For the beta, review how many athletes get from course selection to a measured first split, finish with trustworthy data and voluntarily repeat a climb. Track control failures by qualified model, duplicate saves and high-uncertainty segments. Ask each athlete which section they would train differently and observe whether they can find and repeat it from the debrief.
