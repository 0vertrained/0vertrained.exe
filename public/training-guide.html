Your first working weight
Choose a weight you can control at the bottom of the listed range, with the listed repetitions in reserve (RIR). Log the movement, weight, reps and estimated reserve. The app leaves pounds open when it lacks a comparable performance.
Your next exposure
The runner uses completed sets for the movement you actually performed. For a loaded lift, reaching the top of the rep range on two comparable visits with controlled reps and the intended reserve can earn a small load increase. Otherwise the next target may be familiar weight and one more rep. Compare settings on the same machine. A different exercise gets its own baseline. Both rep progression and load progression have experimental support. In a 12-week squat study, selecting training load by estimated reserve improved strength more than the fixed-load protocol. Plotkin et al. · Graham & Cleather
When recovery changes
A current wearable score or manual check-in, recent training load and fatigue signs can hold progression, reduce today's work, or call for recovery. For example, a 20% reduction on a ten-set session leaves about eight working sets. A 90% working-load target on a recent 100 lb lift is about 90 lb after rounding to the available increment. The adjustment uses the weight you trained with, so no maximum-strength test is required. You can edit the target before logging the set.
When growth stalls
An experienced lifter on a muscle-growth plan can trial one extra set at the same weight after repeated, comparable sessions stall, when recovery and session time allow. Most weeks should keep the same volume while reps or load improve. Volume studies support diminishing returns. The trigger for a trial set and the percentages used on easier days are coaching defaults. Pelland et al. · Barsuhn et al.
What gets saved
Today explains the adjustment. Starting a lifting session or entering a set saves the day's targets alongside the log. You can return to that session later and see the same plan, even if a check-in or your weekly template changes. The next training day uses its own evidence.

What load should I start with?

For muscle growth, start with a weekly set budget and choose a weight you can control. Upper/Lower Hypertrophy begins at ten primary working sets each for chest, back and quads. For example, three sets of dumbbell bench press at 6–10 reps with two reps in reserve means choosing a weight you can press six times while estimating that two more clean reps are possible. If six is a grind, reduce the weight. If ten leaves five more, increase it. Warm-up sets prepare you for those sets and sit outside the budget. Pelland et al., 2026 · Refalo et al., 2024

Weekly volume
In an eight-week trial in trained men, higher set volumes produced more growth at several measured sites. A meta-regression across studies found progressively smaller estimated returns as volume rose. A smaller trial increasing trained lifters’ previous leg volume by 30% or 60% found no clear group difference in size, with substantial attrition. Ten sets is a starting experiment for selected muscles in the growth plan. Training history, recovery and which muscle you measure can all change the response. Schoenfeld et al., 2019 · Pelland et al., 2026 · Barsuhn et al., 2025
Weight and rep ranges
In trained men, lighter and heavier loads taken to failure produced similar muscle-fibre growth over 12 weeks. Heavier loads had an advantage on the bench-press strength test. Across trials, heavy multiple-set programs also ranked well for maximal strength. This supports moderate loads for accumulating growth work and heavier sets of 3–5 on the main strength lifts. A recent one-rep maximum is optional because the rep range and reserve let you find the starting weight directly. Morton et al., 2016 · Schoenfeld et al., 2016 · Currier et al., 2023
Effort and rest between sets
RIR means clean repetitions in reserve. A within-person quadriceps trial observed similar growth after sets taken to failure or stopped at an estimated 1–2 RIR. A broader analysis associated closer proximity to failure with greater hypertrophy, with uncertainty about the exact curve. We use 1–2 RIR on most growth work and 3 RIR for learners. Three minutes on heavy barbell sets helps preserve repetitions. Accessory defaults of 60–120 seconds keep sessions practical. Extend rest if breathing or grip cuts the next set short. Refalo et al., 2024 · Robinson et al., 2024 · Longo et al.
Split and progression
Twice-weekly full-body and four-day split routines produced comparable adaptations in untrained women when weekly work was matched. A separate trial in trained adults found that adding reps or adding load could both drive progress. These plans distribute sets to fit the week. Add clean reps within the listed band, then the smallest available weight once you satisfy the plan’s ceiling rule. Record reps, reserve, load and setup so successive visits are comparable. Pedersen et al., 2022 · Plotkin et al., 2022

Why these weeks are built this way

The studies below tested training principles and selected exercises. The exact weekdays, minute budgets, exercise combinations, and rules for adding a set or easing off are coaching choices. Use the easier entry in each plan when the full prescription exceeds recent training.

Full-Body Foundations · practice the movements
Two ten-set sessions allow novices to repeat leg press, chest press and hip hinge with familiar settings. There are four sets each of pressing, pulling, knee-dominant work and hinging per week, plus trunk and calves. The two-day trial involved untrained women. The machine/free-weight trial found similar changes at measured muscle sites, and a recent elbow-extension trial supports increasing load once a fixed prescription becomes easier. These findings support a simple start and progressive challenge. The exact exercise list and 3 RIR are practical choices. Pedersen et al. · Schwanbeck et al. · Kassiano et al.
Upper/Lower Hypertrophy · spend the sets where you want growth
The week gives chest six press sets and four fly sets, back five row sets and five pulldown sets, and quads ten across leg press, extension, hack squat and split squat. Hamstrings get three hinge sets and five curl sets. There are six calf sets, four trunk sets and four direct sets each for lateral delts, biceps and triceps. Presses and pulls also train the arms. These figures count each listed set for the stated primary target. A volume audit that gives partial credit for secondary movers will show different totals. Weekly volume trial · Dose-response and fractional counting · Machine and free-weight trial
Barbell Strength · practice the lifts you want to test
Squat and bench get six weekly work sets apiece over two exposures. Deadlift gets three, accompanied by two Romanian deadlift sets elsewhere in the week. The priority barbell lift goes first. Low-rep practice with long rests gives the athlete a chance to reproduce setup and force on the measured lifts. The exact allocation is a recovery-budget decision, and a single hard week is a poor reason to add more work. Heavy- versus moderate-load trial · Rest interval trial · Comparative evidence on loading
Bodyweight Progressions · change leverage as you get stronger
Load-matched push-ups and light bench press produced similar results on selected pressing and muscle-size measures in a small eight-week trial. A four-week progressive push-up study supports variation-based progression, and a small six-week squat study in previously inactive women supports gradually harder lower-body variations. This informed the use of inclines, support height and unilateral work. Pulls, sliding curls and holds complete the movement coverage. Evidence for one push-up variation cannot establish the exact dose for a chin-up or plank. Kikuchi & Nakazato · Kotarsky et al. · Wei et al.
Cardio Base · easy volume with one repeatable hard session
The DREW trial found dose-related fitness gains across three exercise volumes in previously sedentary postmenopausal women. Four-minute intervals improved aerobic fitness in trained young women. A small recreational-running study found improvement with two intensity distributions and no clear between-group winner. We combine three conversational sessions and one 4 × 4-minute interval session, for 150 elapsed minutes including easy recovery and 16 hard minutes. The multi-machine mix and one weekly hard session are practical choices. Church et al. · Helgerud et al. · Muñoz et al.
Hybrid Foundations · protect the quality of both sessions
A 16-week concurrent-training trial in untrained men improved aerobic fitness and type-II fibre size. Strength improved more with strength-only training in that sample. Across studies, maximal strength and muscle size generally tolerate concurrent training, while explosive-strength gains can be smaller with both modes in one session. A spacing trial in rugby players also favored separating some exposures. Two lifting days, a 30-minute easy ride and a separate 33-minute interval run leave a recovery day between heavier hinging and faster running. This arrangement is a compromise for a four-visit athlete. Conceição et al. · Robineau et al. · Schumann et al.

Why the road and trail block uses doubles

A race week gives the two goals a deadline. Early weeks give the athlete room to build strength and muscle. As the race approaches, the balance shifts toward arriving with fresh legs. The eight-week calendar, exercise selection and set budgets are coaching decisions informed by the following experiments. See the week-by-week doses and workouts.

Heavy compounds and bodybuilding accessories
In an eight-week trial, adding heavy half squats to trained runners’ usual training improved running economy. A more recent trial in well-trained men found that strength and jump training together improved economy late in a 90-minute run. The block keeps heavy lower-body practice and uses presses, rows and isolation work to train muscle across the week. A trial in trained lifters found similar biceps growth after heavier and moderate-rep programs with matched volume, with greater bench strength gains in the heavier group. Støren et al., 2008 · Zanini et al., 2025 · Schoenfeld et al., 2014
Keep a little in reserve for the next session
In trained adults, eight weeks of leg presses and extensions stopped one or two reps short of failure produced similar average quadriceps growth to sets taken to failure. Failure caused more acute rep and velocity loss. Use the written reserve to choose a demanding weight you can lift cleanly, especially on the squat day that follows a quality run. Refalo et al., 2024
Give each half of a double its own time
In a seven-week study of rugby players, strength gains were smaller when lifting and aerobic work were consecutive than when athletes had six or 24 hours between them. The study scheduled strength first. Putting the quality run first on Tuesday is a race-priority choice. Aim for about six hours between sessions when possible. If that window disappears, keep the priority workout and shorten or drop the second. Robineau et al., 2016
Save the harder running for the quality session
Over five months, a small randomized study of subelite runners found larger gains on a 10.4 km cross-country test when the athletes spent more time at low intensity and similar time at high intensity. Keep easy miles conversational. For road work, choose a repeatable pace on a measured route. On a hill, use breathing and effort to set intensity because the same pace becomes harder as the slope rises. A six-week trial comparing uphill interval formats in trained runners found improved 5 km times on average, with the best interval format uncertain. Esteve-Lanao et al., 2007 · Barnes et al., 2013
Build downhill tolerance before race day
Downhills load the legs as they brake. In a treadmill study, recreational runners had less soreness and a smaller loss of force after a second downhill bout. Use short, familiar descents first and let next-day leg function guide the next exposure. For a trail race, rehearse footing and climbs on similar terrain. For a road race, rehearse even splits on a flat stretch. Match terrain to the event and keep the planned minutes and effort. Khassetarash et al., 2022
Reduce work as the race approaches
A seven-day, lower-volume running taper that retained fast intervals improved 5 km time-trial performance in distance runners. An earlier crossover trial also found longer time to exhaustion after a low-volume taper with some high-intensity running. Those studies support keeping brief race-effort work while cutting accumulated fatigue. The precise taper length and lifting reductions here are programming choices for a runner who also bodybuilds. Houmard et al., 1994 · Shepley et al., 1992

Review a training block

Use six to eight weeks as a practical review window. Keep your first week’s setup and data. Compare the same exercise variation, range of motion and effort. Short trials in specific populations inform the direction of the prescription. They give limited precision about an individual athlete’s best dose. The 2026 ACSM position stand and the volume meta-regression provide broader context for those study limitations. ACSM position stand, 2026 · Pelland et al.

  • Reps, load or duration are improving. Keep weekly sets steady. For growth, optionally compare a circumference under similar conditions over several weeks. A single weigh-in or a post-workout pump tells you little about muscle gain.
  • One muscle or lift has stalled. Check attendance, technique, rest between sets and comparable effort first. A recovered growth-plan athlete can trial one extra weekly set on the stalled muscle. Change one demand and compare the next few exposures.
  • Performance is falling and fatigue lingers. Use the reduction in the selected plan, then reassess. In a hybrid week, trade the hard run for an easy session first if the legs remain heavy. Deload timing and exact reductions are coaching defaults.
  • Check the goal you chose. For barbell strength compare a repeatable submaximal set at similar reserve. For calisthenics record incline, support and reps. For cardio compare pace or power on the same machine at the same perceived effort. For hybrid, track one lifting and one aerobic benchmark independently.

Full-Body Foundations
